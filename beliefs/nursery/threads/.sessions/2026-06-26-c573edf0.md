## The two systems in one line each

- **CB**: durable state is a DAG of *immutable beliefs*; conflicts are an epistemic event you **surface and adjudicate** (preflight → adjudicate → race-detect); raw conversation is distilled into claims+evidence+deps+seed and then *discarded* (nursery evacuates, transcripts vestigial). Storage is plain JSON + git, deterministic, no DB engine, migrating toward one-file-per-belief (cb:a554-560).
- **DeltaDB**: durable state is a stream of *operation-level deltas* with stable identity; conflicts **disappear** via CRDT worktrees; conversation is kept as *primary source material* co-located with the edits it produced.

That framing already tells you where they fit and where they fight.

## Genuine overlap (three axes)

1. **Stable references to moving code.** CB's codepath `code:<path>#<substring>[@N]` anchor exists *specifically* to survive refactors — line numbers aren't stored, the anchor resolves at render time (`CB.CodeLocator`), warning on miss and guessing first-match on ambiguity. DeltaDB's dynamic references solve the identical problem by anchoring to delta identity instead of a string. Same goal, and DeltaDB's mechanism is structurally stronger (no `@N` disambiguation, no render-time warnings).
2. **Immutable, addressable history.** CB: beliefs never edited in place, supersession chains, frozen dated evidence and frozen seeds. DeltaDB: every operation addressable forever. Both are append-only — but CB's grain is *one atomic proposition*, DeltaDB's is *one keystroke-class operation*.
3. **Co-locating reasoning with the artifact.** The nursery makes authorship converse in the concept doc and absorbs the deliberation into the belief's `seed`; DeltaDB records messages beside the edits they caused. Same instinct ("discussion must not drift from the thing"), different object.

## Where DeltaDB is a good fit

**Best fit — codepath anchoring.** This is the cleanest concrete win. If CB's *host code repos* (the ones codepaths point into) lived in DeltaDB, codepath anchors could bind to delta identity rather than substrings. The substring scheme is a clever-but-lossy workaround; DeltaDB makes it refactor-proof by construction. This touches code CB *references*, not the graph itself — low blast radius, real upgrade.

**Second — seed/nursery provenance.** The nursery throws away transcripts but the deliberation still happened in some agent session. A `seed` could be a *navigable DeltaDB reference* into the conversation-plus-edits that produced the concept doc, instead of a frozen prose blob. Provenance becomes traversable rather than narrated — without violating the "nursery holds no archive" rule, because the archive lives in DeltaDB, not the nursery.

**Third (tempting, but see below) — concurrent graph writes.** CB's deepest operational scar is concurrent sessions writing graph repos (commit-hygiene cb:a538, consensus cb:a494/a495, the adjudication race-guard that re-reads before writing, and part of the motivation for per-belief files). DeltaDB's CRDT worktrees are *built* for exactly concurrent multi-agent editing without coordination.

## Where it is not a good fit

**The core conflict: CRDT auto-merge vs. adjudication-by-design.** This kills the "concurrent graph writes" fit above. CB *deliberately refuses* to auto-merge belief writes. A conflicting belief isn't a merge problem — it's an epistemic event that must be bucketed (contract/schema/supportive/neutral) and *decided by a human* (`accept_supersede` / `reject_dep_tie` / `defer`), and the race-guard *refuses to write* if the loser already moved terminal. CRDTs exist to make merge conflicts vanish; CB exists to make them surface and get decided. DeltaDB's signature feature dissolves precisely the friction CB treats as load-bearing. Right for the text layer; actively wrong for the belief layer.

**Grain mismatch → competing SSOT.** CB's immutability is *semantic* (supersession carries reasons, dates, links). DeltaDB's is *mechanical* (operation lineage, meaning-blind). Put beliefs in DeltaDB and you get a second, finer, meaning-blind history beneath CB's meaningful one — two version systems disputing which is SSOT, when cb:a509 is emphatic the graph *is* the durable state.

**Against CB's minimalism.** CB is one runtime dependency (Jason), deterministic traversal, no LLM in the read path, anti-cache (cb:a386) — and the per-belief-files migration is moving *toward* plainer storage (files, not even a cache), not toward a storage engine with a sync server. DeltaDB is a substantial new runtime/storage dependency, and brand-new/unproven. The marginal versioning value over "immutable beliefs + git" is low for the graph (traceable change is already new-belief-plus-link), even while it's high for the host code.

**Opposite stance toward conversation.** DeltaDB treats conversation as primary and preserves all of it; the nursery treats transcripts as vestigial and *discards* them after distilling to a belief. Philosophically crosswise — adopt DeltaDB's disposition wholesale and you'd be re-inflating exactly what the nursery compresses away.

## Bottom line

Split it by layer:

- **Belief graph layer** → poor fit. CRDT merge undermines adjudication, the grain mismatch creates a competing SSOT, and CB's plain-files+git+per-belief-files direction already addresses write contention *while preserving conflict-surfacing*, which is the whole point.
- **Host-code layer (what codepaths anchor into) + provenance capture (seeds)** → good fit. Stable delta references make codepath anchors refactor-proof, and conversation co-location makes seed provenance navigable.

In every overlapping axis the two systems make the *opposite* choice about whether conflict and conversation should be **surfaced and decided** (CB) or **absorbed and preserved** (DeltaDB). That's why the integration story is "DeltaDB underneath the code CB points at," never "DeltaDB underneath the beliefs."
