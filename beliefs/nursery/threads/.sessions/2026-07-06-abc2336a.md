# Composable Beliefs: State, Effectiveness, Design Quality, and the Eval-Ledger Opportunity

## TLDR

The repo is a young (two-week-old extraction, single operator, ~280 commits, 229 authored by Claude), unusually well-engineered framework whose central bet - typed, immutable, source-grounded claims in a git-native DAG with deterministic verification - occupies a lane that is **genuinely empty in the market as of mid-2026**. The eval-ledger positioning is the right one: research across ~25 comps found no shipping product that treats evaluative *conclusions* as first-class, falsifiable, supersedable objects with evidence chains. The main weaknesses are the mirror image of the strengths: near-zero distribution surface (Elixir Mix tasks, no SDK, no benchmark numbers), a flagship graph that underuses its own composition machinery, and a validation story that is entirely self-referential so far.

## 1. State of the repo

- **Codebase**: ~12.7k lines of Elixir across 72 files, one dependency (Jason). 465 tests, all passing. CI gates every push on warnings-as-errors compile, tests, schema self-verification, the belief-to-commit provenance verifier, and generated-doc freshness. That is a stricter hygiene bar than most 10x-larger projects.
- **The graph**: 257 nodes in `beliefs/cb/` - 52 attestations, 9 inferences, 196 prescriptions, essentially zero aggregations. 3 stale nodes, 44 superseded (supersession is genuinely exercised, not decorative), 134 prescriptions awaiting materialized work items.
- **The eval ledger is real, not aspirational**: a versioned run-manifest spec, a deterministic importer (`mix cb.import.eval`) that emits immutable observations only, with hash-derived IDs, idempotent re-import, and hard errors on content changes under an existing run ID; six machine-checkable methodology contracts (cross-ruler corroboration, raw-log provenance, three-run minimum with no escape hatch, LLM-judge human-validation, correction-as-supersession); and a zero-JS self-contained HTML audit-tree renderer, which I smoke-tested successfully.
- **Docs are exceptional for the age**: an eight-chapter guide, a thesis document, a worked end-to-end verdict example, positions, and design records. The doc-to-code ratio is closer to a standards effort than a startup repo.
- **Caveats**: the worked eval collections (`sdl:`, `toy:`, `method:`) live in a sibling repo not present here, so the ledger's usage evidence is one degree removed; and everything has run against exactly one operator's workflow.

## 3. Design quality

High, with a distinctive character. The strongest choices, each of which the research confirms is rare or unique:

1. **Typed epistemic structure.** No comp in any of the three markets distinguishes attestation / aggregation / inference / prescription - i.e. types claims by *epistemic warrant* rather than by function. The academic reviewer's verdict: each piece has lineage (TMS 1979 dependency graphs, AGM 1985 non-numeric revision, nanopublications 2010 citable claims), but the union - plus git-SHA provenance and a self-hosting schema - appears at no single prior system.
2. **Deterministic read path, LLM-free.** Every competitor that handles contradiction does it with an LLM judgment call at ingestion (mem0's ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP, Graphiti's contradiction detection, Letta's sleep-time consolidation). CB's preflight/adjudicate gate and pure-traversal queries are replayable and auditable. This is the design's deepest moat.
3. **The no-confidence stance.** Zep/Graphiti also ships no confidence field, but by omission; CB is alone in arguing it as principle. The 2025-26 academic literature ("confidence laundering," calibration surveys) is fighting exactly the problem CB sidesteps.
4. **Schema-as-contracts-in-the-graph.** Self-describing and machine-verified; nothing found does this.
5. **Discipline about its own boundary.** "Ledger, not lab bench" is held structurally (the importer *cannot* author verdicts), not just in prose. The importer's identity-hashing and refuse-on-rewrite semantics are exactly right for an audit substrate.

Design debts, in order of consequence: no explicit `contradicts` edge type (the claim-auditability literature treats contradiction transparency as first-class, and `cb.adjudicate` is procedural rather than grounded in any argumentation semantics); no retrieval story beyond structural filters (fine at 257 nodes, a real question at 50k); event-time vs transaction-time is conflated (Graphiti's bi-temporal model answers "what was believed true of the world at time T," which CB approximates only via supersession dates); and single-file-per-node in git is unproven at scale.

## 4. As an eval ledger: the comps verdict

This is the strongest part of the story. The eval-tooling research surveyed Braintrust ($800M valuation, Feb 2026), LangSmith, W&B Weave, Arize Phoenix, Langfuse, Inspect, promptfoo (acquired by OpenAI, Mar 2026), Galileo (acquired by Cisco, May 2026), Humanloop, and the GRC platforms (Credo AI, Holistic AI, ModelOp), and concluded:

**Every commercial tool is architecturally a run/trace store with a dashboard on top.** "Model X regressed on Y" exists only as a diff a human eyeballs between two experiment runs - never as an addressable, evidence-linked, falsifiable object that gets visibly superseded when overturned. Nothing on the market records *conclusions* with provenance.

| System | Conclusions first-class? | Immutable record | Supersession | Survives vendor churn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Braintrust | No (dashboards over runs) | Run snapshots only | No | No (SaaS) |
| LangSmith | No | Per-dataset versioning | No | No (LangChain-tied) |
| Inspect (UK AISI) | Partial (typed logs, provenance-tracked log edits) | Yes | Log edits, not claims | Yes (OSS, local) |
| promptfoo / DeepEval | No (pass/fail matrices) | Git-friendly configs | No | Now OpenAI-owned |
| Every Eval Ever / Evaluation Cards (community, Feb 2026) | Partial (reporting-as-claims-making) | Weak, no versioning model | Informal corrections | Community-dependent |
| GRC platforms | No (clause mapping + evidence export) | Doc versioning | No | No (SaaS) |
| **Composable Beliefs** | **Yes** | **Yes, by construction** | **Explicit, machine-checked** | **Yes (JSON in git)** |

Three findings sharpen the case:

- **The durability failure mode is documented, not hypothetical.** Humanloop was acquired by Anthropic in Aug 2025 and fully sunset by September - all customer eval history deleted. A git-native plain-JSON ledger is structurally immune to that.
- **Regulation is demand-side pull for exactly this shape.** EU AI Act record-keeping (10-year provider retention, technical documentation, conformity assessment; high-risk deadlines now Dec 2027 / Aug 2028 after the Digital Omnibus), NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001 audit sampling, and the Fed's SR 26-2 (Apr 2026, superseding SR 11-7) all converge on "dated, evidenced, retained, audit-sampled records of what was tested and concluded." Current answers are GRC evidence-export SaaS, not claims graphs.
- **Independent convergence validates the architecture.** The Kumiho paper (arXiv 2603.17244, Mar 2026) independently built append-only belief revisions, `Supersedes` edges, typed provenance edges, and no confidence scores - and claims novelty for it, which is evidence CB's design point is a live, barely-explored zone rather than settled art. Letta's 2026 pivot to git-backed memory files and EverMind's markdown-first store validate the substrate choice from the memory side, while lacking the epistemic schema entirely.

**What CB would need to add to credibly own the lane** (synthesizing the eval researcher's list with my code reading): adapters that pull structured results from Inspect logs / promptfoo / CI artifacts into the run-manifest (one exists in the sibling repo for Inspect; it needs to be several, and easy); a CI gate that fails a build when a new result contradicts a standing verdict without a supersession; an auditor-facing rendering profile mapped to EU AI Act technical-documentation / ISO 42001 fields; and a non-Elixir on-ramp (the manifest format is already language-neutral - a small Python emitter library would do most of the work).

## 5. Blue ocean potential

**The lane is open; the question is whether it is a lane or a niche.**

- **Red ocean, avoid**: conversational agent memory. mem0 (~60k stars, $24M, AWS default), Zep/Graphiti (28k stars), Letta ($10M seed), LangMem (LangChain, $1.25B), cognee ($7.5M), Supermemory, Hindsight, EverMind, plus OpenAI and Anthropic giving memory away natively. Buyers there select on automatic extraction and LongMemEval-style recall benchmarks - dimensions CB deliberately does not compete on. Analysts already compare the space to the 2022-23 vector-DB market and predict consolidation within 18 months.
- **Blue ocean, real**: the durable, falsifiable record of evaluative conclusions - and more broadly "the system of record for what an organization's agents believe and why." Evidence it is a gap and not a non-market: the academic community is building benchmarks and verifiers (HaluMem, MemConflict, TRUSTMEM) for memory corruption no product addresses; a 2026 survey names "outputs accumulate without versioning, provenance, or linkage to decisions" as the field's unmet need; regulators are writing the requirement into law with dates; and the two closest efforts (EvalEval's Every Eval Ever / Evaluation Cards; Kumiho) are community infrastructure and a single company's internal architecture, respectively - neither productized.
- **The honest risk**: the lane is open partly because the buyer for it barely exists yet. Eval teams currently buy dashboards; compliance teams buy GRC clause-mappers. CB's natural first adopters are frontier-lab eval teams, AI safety institutes, and model-risk functions in regulated finance - a small, sophisticated set that cares about exactly what CB enforces. That is a wedge, not a mass market, and it is a good wedge precisely because incumbents' architectures (mutable run stores) cannot be retrofitted into append-only claims graphs cheaply.

## 6. The load-bearing historical risk

The prior-art research surfaced the one precedent that should be taken most seriously: **the design-rationale capture problem** (Grudin 1988; the fate of gIBIS/Compendium). CB is structurally a rationale-capture system, and that category died because the person paying the authoring cost was never the beneficiary; the only durable deployments hired a dedicated human facilitator. CB's wager is that the LLM-as-author breaks this economics - the same agent that pays the token cost of `/assert` is the future session that benefits from the graph. That is a real structural break, not cosmetic. But the risk relocates rather than vanishes: will an agent under context pressure reliably *choose* the write flow over silently reasoning past it? The repo's own graph contains the evidence that discipline lapses (cb:b386 exists because a digest went stale when agents forgot to regenerate it). The mitigation CB has adopted - move enforcement from procedure into structure (CI gates, verifiers, hooks) - is the correct one, and how far that structural enforcement can be pushed is probably the project's central open question.

## 7. Bottom line

- **Effectiveness**: mechanism proven, verified, and dogfooded; value proposition demonstrated to exactly one user; composition machinery underexercised in its own flagship graph. Call it 7/10 on its stated goals, with the missing points being external validation rather than defects.
- **Design quality**: 8.5/10. Rigorous, self-consistent, unusually honest about its boundaries; the gaps (contradiction edges, bi-temporality, scale, retrieval) are known-class problems, not conceptual flaws.
- **Blue ocean**: high ceiling, narrow door. As agent memory it would be a crowded-market laggard; as an **eval ledger / epistemic system of record** it is, on the evidence gathered, the only shipping thing of its kind, with regulatory tailwinds arriving on dated schedules through 2027-2028. The strategic moves that matter most: publish the audit-tree of a real finding people care about (the marketing artifact is the product), ship a Python run-manifest emitter, and add the CI contradiction gate - those three convert the architecture's uniqueness into something a second user can adopt in an afternoon.
